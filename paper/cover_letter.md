Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "Response of a liquid-jet-anchored oblique detonation to jet interruption, pulsing and throttling," for publication in Physics of Fluids as a regular article.

A transverse liquid jet can initiate and anchor an oblique detonation wave in a fuel spray, but the jets studied so far have all been steady. A practical combustor will throttle or interrupt its injectors, so it matters how an anchored wave responds when the jet changes. The manuscript reports two-dimensional Eulerian–Lagrangian simulations of an n-heptane spray detonation on a 27° wedge, anchored by a jet at momentum ratio J = 1, with the jet switched off, pulsed and throttled. The main findings are:

- After switch-off the detonation persists, and the lower end of the front holds for 11.5–20 µs before retreating. This is about the time the gas takes to cross the first 20 mm behind the front. About 10 µs of the hold remains without jet liquid, and the liquid left near the jet lengthens it.
- Whether pulsing holds the front depends on the duty cycle as well as the frequency, which the stroke ratio of each pulse expresses. At 50% duty, 80 kHz keeps the front within 1.2 mm of the continuous jet, while 20–40 kHz lets it oscillate by 3–4 mm.
- At half the jet fuel, pulsing at 80 kHz holds the front, while throttling the jet to J = 0.25 loses it. The result for a steady half-width jet depends on how the injection is represented.
- Downstream total-pressure recovery does not distinguish a held front from a lost one, and pulsing leaves more fuel vapor unburnt.

The work extends recent Physics of Fluids studies of oblique detonations in sprays and under unsteady inflow to the case of a jet that changes in time. The limits of the study are stated in the manuscript: the simulations are two-dimensional and laminar, each case is a single run, the switching is idealized, and the near-wall spray is dense.
