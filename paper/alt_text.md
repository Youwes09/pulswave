# Alt text

- **Fig. 1.** Two-panel schematic. (a) The full 250 by 100 mm domain on the wedge, with the reduced domain and crop outlined, the jet at x = 50 mm, the measurement plane at x = 100 mm, and the computed and published detonation fronts rising from the jet. (b) Close-up of the front near the jet, with the band at y = 4 to 6 mm that defines the base.
- **Fig. 2.** Line plot of front height against x. The computed fronts at 90 and 100 µs and the reduced-domain front at 70 µs lie 0.5 to 2.8 mm below the published front near the jet and further below it downstream.
- **Fig. 3.** Two line plots against time after switch-off, for the base and for the front at y = 9 mm, on grids of 0.2, 0.15 and 0.1 mm. All grids show a hold and then a retreat, fastest on the finest grid.
- **Fig. 4.** Three color maps of the region near the jet at 70 µs: numerical schlieren showing the jet bow shock and the front, temperature with droplets and the sonic line, and n-heptane vapor mass fraction showing a fuel-rich layer along the wall behind the jet.
- **Fig. 5.** Two line plots. (a) Base position against time: the reference stays near x = 51 mm; with the jet off the base holds and then moves to about 60 mm by 200 µs; with all jet liquid removed it moves sooner; with the jet back on at 95 µs it stops about 3 mm behind the reference. (b) Base displacement against time after switch-off for several runs, crossing the 0.3 mm hold threshold after 10 to 20 µs.
- **Fig. 6.** Four temperature maps near the jet at 0, 10, 25 and 55 µs after switch-off. The front base moves downstream, and by 55 µs a cold region of unburnt gas lies behind the front near the wall.
- **Fig. 7.** Three panels of base position and wall-liquid position against time in the crop, with liquid mass below: the 0.5 mm jet off, 80 kHz pulsing at 25% duty, and the 1 mm jet off. The wall liquid moves downstream faster than the base.
- **Fig. 8.** Two line plots of base position against time in the crop under pulsing. (a) At a fixed off-time of 6.25 µs, 50% duty keeps the base near its start, while 37.5% and 25% duty let it retreat. (b) At 25% duty, all three off-times give a retreat like the jet-off case.
- **Fig. 9.** Four line plots of base position against time for pulsing at 50% duty at 80, 40, 30 and 20 kHz, with jet-off phases shaded. At 80 kHz the base stays close to the reference; at lower frequencies it moves back and forth by 3 to 4 mm.
- **Fig. 10.** Line plot of base position against time for jets with half the fuel. The 80 kHz pulse and the patch-injected half-width jet stay near the reference; the half-velocity jet and the cone-injected half-width jet retreat by about 9 to 11 mm, like the jet-off case.
- **Fig. 11.** Three panels for the measurement plane over 150 to 200 µs. (a) Recovery and (b) burnt fuel, each case minus the reference, as dots per save with the mean for each case. (c) Unburnt vapor per height band: pulsing leaves more vapor, higher above the wall, than the reference.
- **Table I.** List of all simulated cases in the reduced domain and the crop, with jet settings, off-time, jet fuel relative to the continuous jet, and simulated time window.
- **Table II.** Total-pressure recovery, burnt fuel and unburnt vapor on the measurement plane for each case over two time windows, with the number of valid saves.
